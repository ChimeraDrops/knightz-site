Episode 3

Improving Prompts for AI Game Development

How prompts are written to get game-ready results from AI.

Bedwyr's hair took 24 prompt iterations. The one that worked was a screenshot with a blue circle and one sentence. The episode covers what changed between version 1 and version 24: a session-start prompt with context, tasks and a finish line; annotated screenshots for animation fixes; hard limits, gates and fixed reply words that let 15 characters run through review overnight; four mistakes and their fixes; and a loop where every session writes the next prompt.

18 minutes. Published October 14, 2026. Tools: Claude, Blender with Blender MCP, Unreal Engine with Unreal MCP, Filmora.

Episode 2

AI Pipeline Improvement for Game Character Development in Unreal Engine

What the first character pipeline got wrong, what changed, and the cast it produced.

An audit of 20 characters on screen counted 1.9 million triangles before shadows, no LODs, and hair cards stacked 7 to 10 layers deep. The rebuild cut character triangles about in half, turned every flaw into one of six automated hair rules, added a ten-step review pass per hero, and replaced dense hair cards with a shell plus strand cards and one shared garment kit.

19 minutes. Published October 12, 2026. Next episode: Animation.

Episode 1

AI Pipeline for Building Game Characters

From one reference image to a rigged, textured head with hair: Cynan.

The head pipeline runs eight steps: reference, AI mesh, a seam fit to the shared 64-vertex neck, emote topology, a reference sheet, 4K skin, an invisible neck seam and a face rig with 52 ARKit keys. The hair pipeline runs six: a four-view reference, masks and camera fit, a visual hull, a strand atlas, hair cards grown along a flow field, and a final check against the reference.

12 minutes. Published October 5, 2026.
